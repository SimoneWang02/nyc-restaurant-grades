# Behind the A

## What NYC Restaurant Grades Tell You

Every restaurant in New York has to post its letter grade by the door, and many of us use that card to decide where to eat. But the grade is a single letter, and it says very little about what the inspector actually found.

This site looks at what an A really tells a diner: how restaurants earn it, where and for which kinds of restaurants it is harder to get, which violations are behind low scores, and what happens to restaurants that fail. We are not trying to rank restaurants or blame inspectors.

---

## How grading works

Every violation adds points, so a lower score is better, and 0 to 13 points is an A. If a restaurant scores more than 13 on its first inspection, it does not get a grade right away. An inspector comes back, usually within a month, and the second visit decides the grade: 14 to 27 is a B and 28 or more is a C. Inspectors can also close a restaurant on the spot if they find a public health hazard.

So an A on the door can come from a clean first inspection, or from a failed first inspection followed by a better second one.

## Questions

1. How does a restaurant earn its A, and how are first-inspection scores distributed?
2. How much does the share of A grades vary by borough and neighborhood?
3. How much do scores differ by cuisine?
4. Do branches of the same chain get similar grades?
5. Which violations drive scores up, and what share of them are critical?
6. Where do mice, roaches and flies show up most?
7. Do restaurants that miss an A on the first inspection recover at the re-inspection?
8. Which restaurants get closed, and how long does it take them to reopen?

## Data

[DOHMH New York City Restaurant Inspection Results](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Health/DOHMH-New-York-City-Restaurant-Inspection-Results/43nn-pn8j) from NYC Open Data. Health Department inspectors collect it during unannounced inspections. The file covers restaurants that are currently open and roughly their last three years of inspections. We work from a dated copy so our numbers do not shift while we write.
